# Lyrics page

**Sources:** `patch/lyrics.c` (+ `lyrics.h`), the call sites in `patch/nowplaying.c`, `tools/mod_nowplaying.py` (the `np_lyrics` box, the label styles),
`test/pod/lyrics.py`, `LYRICS_PINS` in `tools/pod.py`, `tools/preview_lyrics.py` (the old and the new look side by side over a device screenshot: `uv run python -I tools/preview_lyrics.py out/peek/lyrics-look.png`; `--mode scrim`: the old and the new lyrics scrim over the screenshot and two cover tones; `--mode intro`: four moments of the interlude dots; the layout model, `StbText` on the real font files and the stb rasteriser; AWTK's own wrap is modelled as a greedy word wrap), `tools/mod_font.py` (`lyric_face`) and `tools/font_inter.py` (the Bold subset).

Now Playing's lyrics page (page 2 of the slide view, `view_lyrics`) shows our own lines instead of the stock list. Stages 1 to 4 (this document): the lines, the
current one highlighted from the player position, anchored at 0.35 of the view, opacity by distance, a staggered spring scroll, manual scroll and tap to seek; stage 3 is the Apple Music look: a bold Latin face (a second font file), 30 px with tight leading, white at falling opacity and a fade at the top and bottom edges (the stage 1b look, 28 px Medium in greys, did not read as Apple Music on the device; the motion is unchanged); stage 4 adds Apple Music's interlude dots for the song intro and a lighter scrim for this page. The motion is inspired by
amll-dev/applemusic-like-lyrics (anchor, falloff, staggered spring) without its blur: the X2000 draws on the CPU. **No new hook**: `np_start` calls `ly_start`, the page
timer's `np_work` calls `ly_tick` (500 ms), `np_stop` calls `ly_stop`. Not done: a bigger current line, blur, dots for gaps between lines (the code is ready for them, see Interlude dots), translation, per-word fill.

## What stock does (the probe)

- Two sources, one row list. **Local**: `mclLoadExLyric` `0x5d41e4` reads `<song>.lrc` (or `.LRC`; also `/mnt/mmc/.downpic`, `/tmp/hifi`, `/tmp/.downpic`), iconv to
  `/tmp/templyric`; rows are built at `0x53a240` (`widget_destroy_children(scroll_lrc)`, then a `label_create` per line) and the page timer `0x53cf48` (500 ms)
  scrolls and swaps `navbar_sellyric_style` / `navbar_lyric_style`. **Online**: thread `online_lyricthd` `0x50a048` (kugou, then 163) writes `/tmp/oslyric_<tid>`; the
  500 ms timer `0x537fec` runs `online_loadexlyric` `0x508a00` (parse into the deque `*0xa062e8`, elements `{int32 time; char *text}`; `toolsTimeConvert` `0x5e3020`: `h*3600 + m*60 + s`, so
  **seconds**), builds a `list_item_create` row per line and scrolls (`scroll_view_scroll_to` to `max(0, idx-4)` rows, styles `listitem_sellyric_style` / `listitem_lyric_style`). That timer also handles `/tmp/mmc_add`
  and cover-list work: it stays untouched.
- **Both** put every line in `scroll_lrc` (child of `view_lrc`) as one child widget with the int property **`lrc_time`** and the text (`widget_set_prop(row, "lrc_time", ...)` at `0x5384bc`; the timer
  reads it with `widget_get_prop` at `0x5386a8`). The current line is the last row whose `lrc_time <= get_playtime()` (`0x4e02e4`; stopped, `mclGetPlayStatus() == 1`, is 0: `0x5385cc..0x5385e8`). The unit
  is whatever `get_playtime` counts in (seconds); the payload compares the same two numbers and never converts.
- With no lyrics stock hides `scroll_lrc` and `img_lyricchoice` and shows `label_lyricmsg` ("No lyrics"). The timers look `scroll_lrc`, `label_lyricmsg`, `view_lrc` and `img_lyricchoice` up by name
  every tick and call `widget_set_visible` on them. **Our page therefore reads the stock rows and hides only `view_lrc`**: stock keeps loading, parsing, building and scrolling its (hidden) list; no
  deque, no mutex (`0xa0aaf0`, the local deque's) and no source flag is read; the local and the online source are the same to us. `label_lyricmsg` stays stock's.

## The page

`tools/mod_nowplaying.py` adds one widget to `view_lyrics`, right after `view_lrc`: **`np_lyrics`**, a `scroll_view` on the stock list's rectangle (28, 44, 264, 244: y 44..288 of the window), sensitive (it takes the touches, the payload listens; no axis slidable, so it never scrolls itself),
empty. A `scroll_view` because it **clips its children to its rectangle** (`scroll_view_on_paint_children` sets the canvas clip, `albums.md`); a plain `view` does not clip. Without the payload (kill switch, a failure
below) the stock list shows and `np_lyrics` stays empty. Two label styles join the page theme: `np_lyric_style` (the bold face, `font_name` `lyrics`) and `np_lyric_cjk_style` (the default font), type `label` (a style is found by widget
type and name; the stock `list_item` styles stay for the hidden list), both **`#FFFFFF`, 30 px, left aligned** (`text_align_h` 2 in this build: 0 none, 1 centre, 2 left, 3 right), `spacer` 4 (the row pitch inside a wrapped label), no background; white, neutral, no accent. The current line is not a style any more, only the most opaque line.

`ly_start` (called at the end of `np_start`, after the page timer exists) looks `scroll_lrc`, `view_lrc` and `np_lyrics` up, makes **20 labels and a probe** (`label_create` in `np_lyrics`, 264 x 34: style, `widget_set_sensitive(0)`,
invisible, `label_set_line_wrap`, `word_wrap`), then hides `view_lrc` (`visible=false` and `sensitive=false`). Anything missing or any label refused: `widget_destroy_children(np_lyrics)`, state cleared, the stock list
stays and the page still runs as before. `ly_tick` re-hides `view_lrc` when stock shows it again.

**Lines.** Each tick: `widget_count_children(scroll_lrc)` (at most `LY_MAX` 400) and a signature (count, first and last row handle and time): a change is a new song's lyrics (stock destroys and rebuilds
the rows) and clears everything, rows included. Times and texts are read from the rows (`widget_get_prop_int(row, "lrc_time")`, `widget_get_text_utf8`) only for the lines touched, never copied. The current line is found
from the last one (`ly_find`: the times grow), so a tick is a handful of property reads: an idle tick on 400 lines costs under 40,000 emulated instructions and touches no label. Before the first line's time nothing is current:
line 0 waits at the anchor at 0.45 (the next line), or, for a first line at 4 s or later, one pitch lower under the interlude dots (below).

**Layout.** A line is `rows x 34 px` plus a 12 px gap (46 px for one row). 34 px is what AWTK itself spaces the rows of a wrapped label by: **font size + the style's `spacer`** (30 + 4; the label paint at `0x62e9f8..0x62ea24` reads `spacer`, default 2, and adds it to the canvas font size), so the label of `rows x 34` px holds its rows exactly (stage 2 had the default 2: 30 px rows in a 34 px pitch). `rows` is estimated by measuring the line once in the probe (`widget_measure_text` in the label's own font and style: the probe takes the line's face first): `1 + w / 264`, with `LY_WRAP_SLACK` 56 px (what a word wrap wastes; `LY_WRAP_SLACK_CJK` 8 px in the default font, whose glyphs are all one width) added to a line over one row, at most 3; the label has that height and wraps itself. The slacks were fitted to a greedy word wrap model on 4,000 random lines each (Latin 30 px bold: under-estimates 0.7 %, over-estimates 2.2 %; CJK: none either way). The **anchored line's top is at `LY_ANCHOR` 85 = 0.35 x 244** (plus the manual offset
`man`), the others follow down and up, each plus its spring's offset. Only lines whose place touches 0..244 are placed; the label of line `i` is slot `i % 20` (the 20 lines round the anchor are the window: a move of up to 4 lines can still show the old screen's lines, the test checks the window holds them), and a label is touched only when
its line (text), size, face, opacity or place changed.

**Look (stage 3, Apple Music).** One colour: **every line is white** (`#FFFFFF`); the current line is only the most opaque. **Opacity by distance from the current line: 1.0, 0.45, 0.32, 0.24, 0.18** (255, 115, 82, 61, 46; 0.18 from four lines on; `widget_set_opacity`, which `widget_paint` multiplies into the canvas
alpha, no per-glyph work), times an **edge factor** (`ly_edge`): the label's row nearest the top or bottom edge, by the row's centre, over the last `LY_FADE` 56 px of the view: 0 with the centre on the edge, linear to 255 (1.0) 56 px in, recomputed from the label's y at every placement, so lines fade out as they scroll off at either end, springs and drags included; a label at 0 is hidden. A multi-row label has one opacity, taken from its row nearest the edge. While the user has scrolled, every line but the current one is `LY_BROWSE_OP` 153 (0.6) times the edge factor. The text is **30 px bold, left aligned** in the 264 px label (the page's 28 px margin on both sides), tight leading (34 / 30 = 1.13), no per-line size step; under the text sits the lyrics page's own scrim `np_scrim_lyrics.png` (stage 4: 27 % black, alpha 68, eased 44 row edges: `nowplaying.md`; stage 3 had the info page's 50 % band, which read as a heavy black shadow next to Apple Music's cover colour). (Contrast, worst case over 216 cover tones under the tint and scrim, large text, `test/pod/lyrics.py`: the current line 5.2:1, the next line 2.35:1, then 1.87, 1.6 and 1.43: the falloff is meant to recede, the numbers are guarded as floors 4.5 / 2.3 / 1.8 / 1.55 / 1.4; at the 50 % scrim they were 8.86 / 3.19 / 2.37 / 1.92 / 1.55, and 64 instead of 68 would put the next line at 2.29. The falloff opacities are unchanged. The `nowplaying.md` contrast test covers the other styles.)

**Faces (stage 3).** AWTK draws a glyph from one font only: `font_manager_get_font` (`0x650dc4`) finds a font by the style's `font_name` (the file `assets/default/raw/fonts/<name>.ttf`, loaded by `assets_manager_load_file`), and if that file cannot be loaded retries with the default font name (`0x650ebc`); a glyph the chosen font lacks is skipped, there is no per-glyph fallback. So there are two label styles and the payload picks per line: **`np_lyric_style`** (`font_name` `lyrics`: `lyrics.ttf`, **Inter Bold 4.1** as a 153 KB second font file, the same subset as `Inter-m1plus.ttf` (`assets/fonts/Inter-Bold-m1plus.ttf`, `tools/font_inter.py --weight Bold`, 1,429 code points, OFL kept), tracked -16 units, default.ttf's unitsPerEm and vertical metrics so 30 px is the same pixels and the baseline the same: `fonts.md`) for a line whose code points are **all** in that file (`ly_latin`: Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, Vietnamese, punctuation, currency; a table of 34 ranges the test checks against the file's cmap), and **`np_lyric_cjk_style`** (no `font_name`: the default font, Source Han Sans Medium for CJK, kana and Hangul and Inter Medium for the Latin in them) for any other line, malformed UTF-8 included. The face of a line is chosen when it is measured (`ly_rows`, cached with the rows), the label's style is set only when its line changes face, and the probe takes the same style before it measures. A line mixing CJK and Latin is drawn whole in the default font.

**Size (stage 3).** 30 px is the largest size whose three-row current line at the anchor plus the next line's row keep 8 px inside the 244 px view (rows 34 px, gap 12: 85 + 3 x 34 + 12 + 34 = 233; 31 px would add 4 px to the four rows: 237), and the cap stays 3 rows. Measured with `StbText` on the real files (advance truncated, +1 px a glyph; width in px at 30 px): `Be what you want to be` 263 (one row of 264, just), a 61-character Latin line 609 (3 rows with the slack), a 26-character Chinese line 754 and Japanese line 676 (3 rows each); a 100-character Latin line and a 36-character CJK line are clipped at row 3, the price of the size. The test pins the samples and the fit rule.

**Motion (stage 2).** When the anchored line moves from `a` to `b` (`ly_move`; the song moved on, the user's 3 s are over, a tap) every line stays where it was on the screen: the pixel distance `D`
between the two anchors, the manual offset included, is added to what each line still has (`o0`, a start offset, and `ts`, its start time, per label slot), and the line's **spring** brings it to 0.
**Spring** (`ly_spring`): a damped spring, zeta 0.72, wn 12 /s, 1024 x `e^(-zeta wn t)(cos wd t + zeta / sqrt(1 - zeta^2) sin wd t)` every 10 ms in a 65 entry table (`ly_tab`; the test computes the same
numbers from the formula), linear between entries, integer maths and no floats, 640 ms long: it overshoots by at most 3.8 % of the distance (a pitch of 46 px: under 2 px; a three-row line, 114 px: 4 px) and the overshoot is cut at
`LY_OVERSHOOT_MAX` 6 px, so nothing leaves the view; at 640 ms it is -0.1 %. **Stagger** (`ly_delay`): line `i` starts `delay(i - (b - 1))` ms after the move, 0 for the lines above the one before the target,
then 50 ms, each step 20/21 of the one before (50, 97, 141, 182, ...: AMLL's 1/1.05), so the lines settle one after the other, top first. One **40 ms timer** (`ly_frame`) exists while any line still has a spring running and
ends itself (`RET_OK`); `ly_stop` removes it. A frame only moves labels (the ones entering the window get their text): at most 9 `widget_move` calls. A move of more than `LY_SNAP_LINES` 4 lines (a seek, late lyrics, a snap-back from
far) jumps; a refused timer jumps too. A new move in the middle of another starts each line from where it is.

**Touch (stage 2).** `np_lyrics` takes the touches; the labels stay insensitive (the stock list is hidden: `visible=false` + `sensitive=false`, hidden widgets are still hit-tested). `ly_start` binds **listeners** (`widget_on`)
for DOWN, MOVE, UP and ABORT on it; they run after the `scroll_view`'s own handler, which has no slidable axis (so it never aborts or scrolls) and answer `RET_OK` always: nothing is grabbed, so the slide view and the window manager see
every event as on the info page. The stock `scroll_view` gesture hook (`gestures.md`) does nothing for it (no animator, no slidable axis).
- **Drag**: a vertical move of 10 px (`LY_TAP_PX`) that is also larger than the sideways one starts it; from then on the lines follow the finger (`man`, re-based so that `|man|` stays under half a pitch, the first line
  stopping at the anchor from below and the last from above). The first 10 px decide the gesture and are not scrolled. A move that goes **sideways** first (10 px) leaves the touch to the slide view: the page turns, no scroll and no tap; its ABORT ends ours.
  While scrolled the lines do not follow the song (the highlight does) and every other line is at 0.6 (times the edge factor).
- **Snap back**: `LY_IDLE_MS` 3000 after the last touch event (DOWN, MOVE, UP or ABORT; checked by the 500 ms page tick, so 3.0 to 3.5 s), the lines move back to the current one with the same spring and stagger.
- **Tap**: a touch that ends within 10 px of where it began, was not a drag and began outside the first `LY_COAST_MS` 200 ms of a move (the 200 ms touch rule of `gestures.md`: a touch on a line that has only just started moving catches it and
  presses nothing; later in the 640 ms the lines are almost still and a tap works) seeks to the line under it (`widget_to_local` for the box coordinates; a line's rows plus half its gap) with the stock **`seek_time(seconds)`**
  `0x4e0544` (the call the Now Playing slider and the hardware keys make: it adds the playing record's CUE start, `mclSetSeek`, resumes a paused player and waits until `get_playtimeandlength` is within a second, at most 2 s; a stopped player,
  `mclGetPlayStatus() == 1`, does nothing and the tap does nothing). `lrc_time` is in the unit of `get_playtime`, the same one `seek_time` takes (seconds). The tapped line becomes current and the lines move to it at once; the tick leaves them
  alone for `LY_HOLD_MS` 1000 so the player's position can catch up (otherwise the old position would pull them back).
- **The stock row tap is gone**: the stock rows' click handler `0x536e1c` does not seek; it flips the lyric display flag (`0xa00000 + 0x6d30`, `lbu` @`0x536e3c`, `sb` @`0x536e50`) and refreshes the page. The stock rows are hidden and
  never hit, so that toggle is no longer reachable from a tap on the lyrics; nothing else read or changed.

## Interlude dots (stage 4)

Before the first line, while the song's intro plays, three dots show at the anchor row, left aligned at the text (Apple Music's pause dots). They show only when the first line's `lrc_time` is at least `LY_DOT_MIN_S` **4 s** (a shorter intro is no gap worth showing). While they show the lines sit **one pitch (`LY_DOT_PAD` 46 = 34 + 12) lower** than the anchor: line 0 is the first of the *upcoming* lines (opacity 0.45, then 0.32, ... as always, edge fade included), and the dots take the anchor row (`ly.pad`, added to the anchor in `ly_layout` and `ly_move`).

- **Dots.** `np_dot.png`, a **10 px** white disc, anti-aliased (8 x 8 samples a pixel; `mod_nowplaying.dot_image`; 10 px is crisp and the smallest that reads as a circle on this 320 px screen), shown by three `image` widgets that `ly_start` makes in `np_lyrics` after the label pool (`image_create`, `image_base_set_image("np_dot")`, insensitive, hidden): x `LY_DOT_X` 2 + k x `LY_DOT_PITCH` 18, y so that the dot centres on the anchor row (`ANCHOR + (34 - 10) / 2` = 97 from the top of the box when settled). A dot is placed from line 0's displayed top (`ly_dots`: that top minus `LY_DOT_PAD`), so the dots ride with the first line through a spring, a drag (a drag of the lines moves the dots) and the edge fade. No dot image is a tap target; a touch on them is a touch on `np_lyrics`. An asset image was chosen over a glyph (Inter has no U+25CF) and over a styled view (`round_radius` on a view is unverified on the device, the slider's was an open question too) because the existing image path (`image_create`, used by Home and Cover Flow) is known to draw and the opacity multiplies in as it does for labels.
- **Fill and breath** (`ly_dot_op`, the Python model `dot_op`; all constants in `offsets.inc`). With `el` ms into the gap of `len` ms, dot k fills from `LY_DOT_DIM` 64 (0.25) to 255 as `el` passes the k-th third of `len - LY_DOT_FADE_MS` (so dot 0 is full by a third of the way, dot 1 by two thirds, and dot 2 as the fade starts), times a **breath**: a triangle wave of `LY_DOT_BREATH_MS` 2400 ms between 255 and `LY_DOT_BREATH_MIN` 160 (0.63) on the whole group, taken from `el`, so it freezes with the player (a scale is not available on an AWTK widget; a size step would need several images per dot and was left out), times a **fade out** over the last `LY_DOT_FADE_MS` 500 ms to 0, so the dots are gone when the first line becomes current. The lyrics then move in with the existing spring and stagger (pad 46 -> 0 is a move of the lines by 46 px: `ly_move` adds `pad - new pad` to `D`).
- **Clock.** `get_playtime` returns **whole seconds** (`mclGetPlayTime`'s `lrc_time` unit; the intro of 10 s moves in 1 s steps), too coarse for a fill. `ly_clock` interpolates with `time_now_ms` (the payload's monotonic clock, already imported): a first read or a seek is taken as the middle of the second (`pos x 1000 + 500`), a second that has just turned is its start (`pos x 1000`), between turns the elapsed time is added while the status is playing (2; not across a pause) and never past `pos x 1000 + 999`. The error is under a second after a seek and a poll interval (80 ms) after the first turn; the position only ever moves forward between seeks. The lyric line itself is still started by the integer position (`ly_find`), as before: the dots finish exactly as that second starts.
- **One short timer** (`ly_dframe`, `LY_DOT_MS` 80 ms) exists only while the dots show and the player plays, and ends itself (`RET_OK`) when they are gone or the player pauses; the 500 ms page tick starts it (`ly_dkick`). A frame runs the same `ly_poll` as the page tick (status, position, clock, current line, dots' opacities), then the layout, so the position, the highlight and the dots always agree; the frame polls `get_playtime` 12 times a second (25 times the stock rate, only during an intro; a call is a few reads of the player's state, `get_playtime` `0x4e02e4` is `mclGetPlayPos` plus `mclGetPlayTime`). A refused timer leaves the dots to the page tick: they show and move twice a second, never stuck.
- **Cases** (all in the tests). Pause: the timer ends, the dots stay at their last opacity, the page tick keeps them still, and on play the clock continues from where it froze. Stopped player: position 0, dots dim, no timer. Seek into the intro (from a later line, within it): the dots come back at the new fill, the lines move down under them (a jump when far); out of it: the first line is current, the dots are gone at once. Song change (the stock rows are rebuilt): everything is cleared, the dots take the new gap and the clock starts over. Lyrics arriving late: during the intro the dots show and the lines sit under them with no move, later nothing shows. No lyrics, kill switch, a refused `image_create` (no dots, the first line at the anchor), a refused timer: see above, never a stuck UI. While the user has scrolled, the lines and dots stay under the finger when the first line starts (the pad change is taken out of `man`).
- **Mid-song gaps (not enabled).** The intro is one case of "a gap with no line current": `ly_gap_len()` returns its length in ms (0: none), `ly_dot_op(k, el, len)` and `ly_clock`'s `est` are about the gap, not the song, and `ly_dots(have0, d0)` places the dots from a line's displayed top. To show them in a long gap between lines i and i+1 (say a pause of 8 s or more), `ly_gap_len` would return `time(i+1) - time(i) - end of line i` for `cur == i` with the elapsed time `est - time(i) x 1000` passed as `el`, the pad would apply to the lines *after* i (the dots sit under line i, line i+1 and the rest move down by one pitch: `ly_layout` would add `pad` to the nominal places from `i + 1` on and `ly_dots` would take line i+1's top), `ly_want_pad` would return it for `cur == i` too, and `ly_move` already moves the lines by the pad's change. Nothing of that is wired: only `cur < 0` shows dots.

## Safety

Runs from the page timer, the animation timer and the pointer listeners of `np_lyrics` (all the UI thread); no allocation, no thread, no lock, no stock memory written, no paint code. A tap blocks the UI thread for as long as `seek_time` does (the stock slider's path: a few tens of ms, 2 s at most). The kill switch, a missing widget, a refused label, a refused timer each leave the stock
list (or jump instead of easing). `np_lyrics` is the only widget name added; no stock name is renamed or removed.
